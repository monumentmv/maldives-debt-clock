What MIRA collects, and how much is paid in dollars

Maldives Inland Revenue Authority (MIRA) collects most of the government's taxes, along with fees and rents such as resort land rent. A large part is paid in US dollars, mainly by resorts and other tourism businesses. Those dollars matter for the debt, because loans from abroad have to be repaid in foreign currency.

Reading these figures

These figures come from MIRA's monthly revenue collection files. One lists everything MIRA collected, and the other lists the part paid in US dollars. MIRA isn't the only collector. Import duties are collected by Maldives Customs Service, and the government also receives dividends from state companies, other income and grants, so these totals are smaller than all government revenue.

MIRA's rufiyaa figures already include the dollar payments, converted at MIRA's own rate, which is slightly below MMA's reference rate. This page converts the dollar figures at each month's MMA reference rate. For taxes paid almost entirely in dollars, such as tourism GST and green tax, that can come out a little above MIRA's own rufiyaa total, so the dollar part of each tax is capped at that total. Dollar figures start in January 2011.

Resort land and leases includes one-off payments. Lease period extension fees, paid by resorts to extend their island leases, jump in some years and fall back in others, so a big year for this line is not a sign of lasting growth. The compare table and the chart pop-ups show them separately. Tourism tax was a fixed charge for each tourist night that ended in 2014. Tourism GST began in 2011 and is now the largest single tax.

Comparisons between periods follow the Real switch. With Real on, both periods are shown in today's prices, so the change reflects growth after inflation. The latest figures at the top are always in the money of the time. MIRA updates these files around the middle of each month, and recent months are provisional until MIRA reconciles them.
