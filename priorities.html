What interest costs, next to what keeps people safe

Interest pays lenders for borrowing that has already been done. It doesn't build a school, run a hospital or pay a police officer. This page sets it beside what the government spends on policing, justice and protecting people's rights.

Where interest goes

Interest is income for whoever lent to the government. That makes it different from almost everything else in the budget.

Lenders in Maldives

Commercial banks, MMA, the pension fund and other financial institutions hold most Treasury bills and bonds. Their interest stays in the economy, but it goes to lenders rather than public services.

Lenders abroad

Interest on external loans, sukuk and international bonds leaves the country in foreign currency and draws on reserves.

What it crowds out

Every rufiyaa paid in interest is a rufiyaa the budget can't spend on policing, schools, hospitals or anything else that year.

Reading these figures

Spending and interest come from the Ministry of Finance's weekly fiscal reports. Each figure is the total from 1 January to the date of the report. Interest is what the Ministry reports as financing and interest costs, and doesn't include loan repayments. Weekly figures are early and change as accounts are reconciled.

Case counts are cases reported to the Maldives Police Service. They count reports, not convictions, and include traffic accidents and lost items. Placing an office next to a type of case shows scale only.

Office names change as ministries are merged or split, so the same office can appear under different names in different years. Courts aren't shown because judiciary spending moved into the Special Budget in April 2026. More in the methodology.
