What Maldives spends on imported fuel

Maldives imports almost all of its energy as petroleum products. Diesel accounts for a large share of fuel use, generating most of the country's electricity and powering many road vehicles, boats and fishing vessels. Petrol is also widely used in cars, motorcycles and smaller boats, while aviation fuel, cooking gas and other petroleum products make up the remainder. When wars or other global crises push up oil prices, the country's fuel import bill rises with them, and the foreign currency spent on these imports flows out of the economy.

    Reading these figures

    Fuel import values come from Maldives Customs Service (MMA series 3503, with petrol 3504, diesel 3505 and other products 3507). The oil price is the World Bank's monthly average of Brent, Dubai and WTI crude (series 3787). Fuel is bought and recorded in US dollars, so the charts and crisis comparisons stay in dollars. That keeps the history undistorted. The rufiyaa was fixed at 12.80 per dollar until March 2011, then moved to about 15.40, so converting the history to rufiyaa would add a jump that has nothing to do with fuel. Only the headline figures at the top follow the MVR/US$ switch, converted at each month's MMA reference rate.

    Break in April 2015. After duty on petroleum products was reinstated from 1 April 2015, the series only includes fuel sold within Maldives. Earlier months also include fuel that was not sold locally, so values before and after the dotted line are not fully comparable.

    The import bill reflects both prices and quantities. A higher bill can mean fuel cost more, or that the country bought more of it, for example when tourism grows. The inflation adjustment ("Real") isn't applied on this page because fuel is priced in US dollars, and Maldivian consumer prices aren't the right measure for that. More in the methodology.
