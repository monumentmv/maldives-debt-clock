Where interest goes

Interest is income for whoever lent to the government. That makes it different from almost everything else in the budget.

Lenders in Maldives

Commercial banks, MMA, the pension fund and other financial institutions hold most Treasury bills and bonds. Their interest stays in the economy, but it goes to lenders rather than public services.

Lenders abroad

Interest on external loans, sukuk and international bonds leaves the country in foreign currency and draws on reserves.

What it crowds out

Every rufiyaa paid in interest is a rufiyaa the budget can't spend on policing, protection or anything else that year.

Reading these figures

Spending comes from the Ministry of Finance's Weekly Fiscal Developments, table 5, budget by office. Interest is the line "Financing and Interest Costs", which the Ministry counts within recurrent spending. Loan repayments are not included. Weekly figures are early and change as accounts are reconciled.

Days of interest divide an agency's spending by the average daily interest cost over the same period. Matching an agency with a type of case shows scale only. It doesn't mean the agency handles every such case, or that spending and cases are linked.

Case counts are cases reported to the Maldives Police Service, taken from its crime statistics page. They count reports, not convictions, and include traffic accidents and lost items. Older years may be undercounted if fewer cases were recorded on the police reporting platform.

Courts aren't shown because judiciary spending moved into the Special Budget in April 2026. Gender and family functions were merged into other ministries in April 2026 and aren't reported separately. The National Drug Agency first appears as its own office in 2024. More in the methodology.
